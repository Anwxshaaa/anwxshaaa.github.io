About Me

Who am I, academically?

I did my BSc Physics (Hons) from the University of Delhi, before moving to the University of Glasgow for my MSc in Astrophysics.

Somewhere along this path, I became more interested in the physics and instrumentations that help us listen to the Universe as well (how cool is that)!!

My MSc dissertation combined two things I really enjoy: understanding the physics behind an idea and then actually getting my hands on the experiment. I worked with a tabletop Michelson interferometer to investigate how environmental disturbances couple into an interferometric system, alongside noise modelling and interferometric simulations using PyGWinc and FINESSE.

Since then, my interests have increasingly moved towards gravitational-wave instrumentation, interferometry, optomechanics, photonics and control systems. I’m simply awestruck with the idea that something as seemingly simple as light travelling between two mirrors can actually listen to the Universe telling us its secrets!

NOW, who am I outside academia?

Beyond equations & codes, I’m obsessed with books (mostly romances)! And I’ve a soft spot for stories that make me feel too much about fictional characters! And sometimes I believe a good book says something about real life much better than real life does!

Add to this, I love writing, exploring new places, hunting crooks and crannies of new streets for some yum food, and am obsessed with sky for no particular scientific reasons!

This website is therefore a little bit of both: the scientist I am becoming and the person I am when I am not thinking about interferometers (but still thinking about it a little bit!!!)

Research & Projects

Sensing & Control of Radiation-Pressure Dominated Cavity

Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics (IUCAA)
Pune, India
Remote Research Student
Supervisor: Dr. Manasadevi P Thirugnanasambandam

I am currently working on sensing and control in radiation-pressure-dominated cavities for gravitational-wave detector applications. Using FINESSE 3, I apply harmonic demodulation techniques to extract sensing signals and construct the sensing matrix for the cavity’s different degrees of freedom. I use the diagonal dominance of the sensing matrix, indicating weak cross-coupling between degrees of freedom, as a baseline for assessing the system. The resulting model is then used to investigate the effects of radiation pressure on cavity dynamics and its stability.

Laser Interferometry for Gravitational Wave Detection

University of Glasgow Glasgow, UK
MSc Thesis
Thesis Supervisor: Dr. Andrew Spencer

The main aim was to design and construct a tabletop, open-loop Michelson Interferometer and under how various environmental disturbances couple into the interferometric output under various operating conditions.

Alongside, to get more idea about the detector design perspectives, I used PyGWinC to model noise budgets and explored how changing key design parameters including, laser power, arm length, optics affects the overall sensitivity. I also used FINESSE 3 to simulate the tabletop Michelson interferometer and investigate the properties of the system we built experimentally.

Overall, the aim was to connect the theoretical picture of detector noise with what we actually observe in an experiment.
